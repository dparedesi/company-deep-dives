# Palantir Technologies (PLTR) - Customers and Use Cases Research

## Overview

This document compiles comprehensive research on Palantir's customer base, use cases, and deployment metrics across government and commercial sectors. The research covers Palantir's evolution from its intelligence community roots to its current position as a multi-platform software company serving defense agencies, national health systems, and Fortune 500 commercial enterprises.

The data reveals a dramatic transformation in Palantir's customer profile and go-to-market approach since 2023. The launch of AIP (Artificial Intelligence Platform) and the boot camp model has accelerated commercial adoption, with US commercial customer count growing from 143 (2022) to 262 (Q1 2024) and revenue growth exceeding 120% year over year by Q3 2025. Government business has deepened through major contract wins including a $1 billion Department of Homeland Security contract and the designation of Maven Smart System as a Pentagon programme of record.

Key customer categories include: (1) Defense and intelligence agencies with long-term classified contracts; (2) Federal civilian agencies including DHS and ICE with controversial enforcement deployments; (3) National health systems with quantified outcome metrics; (4) Commercial enterprises across aerospace, automotive, energy, healthcare, financial services, and manufacturing; and (5) Partner ecosystem including consulting firms and defense technology companies.

## Detailed Findings

### Government Customer Base

**Intelligence Community Origins and Core Relationships:**

Palantir's foundation was built on deep relationships with US intelligence agencies. The company's earliest and most enduring customer is reportedly the Central Intelligence Agency (CIA), which provided both initial funding through In-Q-Tel and early operational validation. Palantir Gotham software was reportedly used in the operation that located Osama bin Laden, though specific details remain classified. This intelligence community pedigree remains central to Palantir's identity and serves as a reference for other government customers.

The Federal Bureau of Investigation (FIA) has utilized Palantir for case management and investigative analysis, integrating data from crime reports, arrest records, public records, tips, and license plate readers. While specific contract values and outcomes are not publicly disclosed, the longevity of these relationships indicates sustained perceived value.

**Defense Department Contracts and Programmatic Status:**

Palantir's defense business has evolved from discrete contracts to programmatic status, representing a significant milestone in government customer relationships. The US Army's Vantage Program provides a data-driven platform for assessing personnel readiness, equipment availability, and training status across the force. A $250 million contract expansion in May 2024 to accelerate AI and machine learning capabilities underscores perceived value.

The TITAN (Tactical Intelligence Targeting Access Node) program represents another strategic milestone, with a Palantir-led consortium selected as prime contractor. TITAN aims to use AI/ML to rapidly process sensor data from space, air, and ground for long-range precision fires, positioning Palantir at the center of the military's sensor-to-shooter architecture.

**Maven Smart System - Pentagon Programme of Record:**

Maven Smart System, which uses Palantir technology, has been designated as a formal Pentagon programme of record. This designation elevates Maven from a prototype or experimental initiative to an established, funded program with sustained budgetary support. This is a significant milestone for Palantir's defense business and validates the Operational Sovereignty thesis at scale. Programme of record status provides revenue stability and reduces renewal risk compared to discretionary contracts.

**Department of Homeland Security $1 Billion Contract:**

In early 2026, Palantir secured a contract with the US Department of Homeland Security valued at approximately $1 billion. This represents a significant expansion of Palantir's footprint in federal homeland security operations. The contract demonstrates DHS's commitment to Palantir technology for missions including border security, cybersecurity, and emergency response. The $1 billion scale places this among Palantir's largest publicly disclosed government contracts.

**ICE ImmigrationOS and $30 Million Contract:**

Palantir holds an ImmigrationOS contract with US Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) valued at approximately $30 million. The system has been reported as powering aspects of the US deportation crackdown. This contract crystallises the tension between Palantir's government work and ethical concerns around surveillance and civil liberties. The ImmigrationOS deployment has drawn criticism from technology figures including Paul Graham and civil liberties organizations. The contract represents ongoing revenue but also reputation risk in the commercial market.

**US Military AI Enterprise Consolidation:**

The US military is moving toward an "AI enterprise" architecture that consolidates previously fragmented contracts into larger umbrella agreements. Palantir is positioned as one of the core software vendors in this consolidation. AIP serves as an orchestration layer on top of Gotham data that can automate tasks such as triaging sensor feeds, recommending logistics moves, or proposing maintenance actions. This represents a shift from purely analytical systems to ones that can act semi-autonomously under human supervision.

**NATO and Allied Defense Adoption:**

Palantir's defense customer base extends beyond the US military to NATO allies. Maven Smart System deployments across US and NATO forces demonstrate alliance-wide adoption. This international defense footprint provides geographic diversification and reduces dependence on any single government's procurement cycles.

### National Health Systems and Public Sector

**NHS Federated Data Platform - Comprehensive Metrics:**

The NHS Federated Data Platform (FDP) represents Palantir's largest and most measurable public sector deployment. Palantir serves as the core technology provider under NHS England governance. By late September 2025, 77 NHS hospital trusts were live and reporting benefits, with 150 trusts having signed memoranda of understanding to join, plus all 41 integrated care boards.

The FDP has delivered quantified system-level outcomes across four major solution areas:

1. Inpatient Care Coordination Solution: Enabled approximately 80,923 additional patients to be treated in theatres through better scheduling and waiting list management. A further 77,206 patients have been safely removed from inpatient waiting lists where treatment was no longer clinically needed.

2. Outpatients Care Coordination Solution: Facilitated 213,101 patients being safely requested for removal from outpatient waiting lists.

3. Referral to Treatment (RTT) validation tool: Reviewed over 2.2 million records, resulting in 563,239 people being removed from waiting lists where care was no longer required.

4. OPTICA discharge product: Achieved a 14 to 15 percent reduction in delayed discharge days for long-stay patients, with more than 206,000 patients discharged using OPTICA-supported workflows.

These figures illustrate "operational sovereignty" at health-system scale. The shared ontology enables multiple micro-workflows to compound: theatre scheduling, waiting list validation, discharge coordination, and bed management all operate on the same data foundation.

However, the FDP has generated substantial opposition. The British Medical Association voted in 2025 to oppose the rollout and lobby for termination of existing Palantir contracts, citing privacy, governance, and trust concerns. A 2024 BMJ editorial titled "NHS England must cancel its contract with Palantir" exemplifies how even measurable operational gains are being weighed against perceived sovereignty and ethical risks.

**Tampa General Hospital - Commercial Healthcare Metrics:**

Tampa General Hospital (TGH) has become one of the best quantified commercial case studies for Palantir's healthcare applications. The academic medical center created a "Care Coordination Center" (CareComm) using Foundry and AIP to optimize patient flow, manage staffing levels, reduce emergency department wait times, and improve overall hospital operational efficiency.

Recent data shows transformative results:
- 83 percent reduction in patient placement time
- 28 percent decrease in post-anesthesia care unit (PACU) holds
- 30 percent reduction in sepsis length of stay

TGH won the Joint Commission's 2025 Healthcare Innovation Award partly for this work. The CareComm system exemplifies AIP and agentic workflows in healthcare: recommending bed assignments, proposing staffing changes, and escalating at-risk patients rather than simply displaying dashboards.

**COVID-19 Pandemic Response:**

During the COVID-19 pandemic, Palantir's platforms were used by various government health agencies globally including the US Department of Health and Human Services and the UK's National Health Service. Applications included integrating data on infection rates, hospital capacity, testing, and patient outcomes; managing the supply chain for critical medical equipment; supporting vaccine distribution logistics; and forecasting disease spread. These pandemic-era deployments evolved into longer-term commercial and public sector relationships, most notably the NHS FDP.

### Commercial Customer Base

**AIP Boot Camp Model and Customer Acquisition:**

Since late 2023, Palantir has fundamentally shifted its commercial entry motion through AIP boot camps: free, intensive workshops where customer teams build working agents on their own data in days rather than months. This approach has driven remarkable acceleration in commercial adoption.

Key metrics:
- US commercial customer count: 143 (end of 2022) → 221 (end of 2023) → 262 (Q1 2024)
- AIP boot camps conducted: approximately 324 (Q4 2023) → 660 (Q1 2024)
- Boot camp to paying customer conversion: 5 to 10 percent within 3 to 6 months
- US commercial revenue growth: approximately 40% year over year through 2023/early 2024, exceeding 120% year over year by Q3 2025
- Remaining deal value: nearly tripled

This model has two critical implications. First, "time to first value" has collapsed from months or years to weeks. Second, the boot camp approach empowers users themselves to build workflows rather than relying entirely on Palantir's Forward Deployed Engineers, changing both cost structure and adoption curve.

**Aerospace and Industrial Customers:**

Airbus co-developed Skywise with Palantir, a data platform built on Foundry for the aviation industry. Applications include predictive maintenance to reduce unplanned downtime, fuel efficiency optimization, operational performance improvement, and manufacturing optimization. One airline using Skywise reportedly improved fleet utilization by 7 percentage points. Airbus also uses Foundry internally for manufacturing processes and supply chain optimization.

Lowe's is working with Palantir to build a supply chain digital twin that optimizes inventory and replenishment, moving from batch planning to near real-time optimization. This represents a commercial example of an ontology plus agents managing a large logistics network.

Jacobs uses Foundry and AIP for its OneWater platform, building predictive models for wastewater and drinking water systems. Agents can propose pump setting changes or alert operators to upcoming shortages, with improved ability to predict overflows and plan preventive interventions.

**Automotive and Mobility:**

Stellantis uses Foundry for supply chain management and resilience, improving visibility across complex multi-tier global supply chains, identifying potential disruptions, and optimizing inventory. Palantir extended this partnership for five years in early 2026, demonstrating continued commercial traction.

Scuderia Ferrari uses Palantir to rapidly analyze telemetry and sensor data from F1 cars and race simulations to optimize car performance, race strategy, and real-time decision-making. In a sport where milliseconds matter, data analytics provides competitive advantage.

Hertz has chosen AIP to modernize fleet analytics and AI development, replacing legacy systems. Goals include optimizing fleet allocation, pricing, and maintenance using generative AI and agents working over vehicle, customer, and operational data.

**Energy and Resources:**

Kinder Morgan uses Foundry to give gas control teams a unified real-time view of pipeline networks. Tasks that used to take hours per day can now be done in minutes, and engineers can run simulations of outages or new loads directly on the model.

BP historically used Palantir across upstream (exploration and production), midstream (pipelines), and downstream (refining) operations. However, BP notably scaled back its usage, indicating that perceived long-term value and cost can shift. This reduction represents a rare public example of a customer disengaging.

Applications in energy include integrating geological and seismic data to optimize well placement, managing pipeline and transportation networks, optimizing refinery operations, and managing renewable energy performance.

**Healthcare and Life Sciences:**

Beyond Tampa General Hospital, Sanofi uses Foundry to accelerate R&D processes, optimize clinical trials, improve manufacturing quality and yield, and enhance supply chain visibility for pharmaceuticals. A major healthcare payer signed a five-year, $26 million deal within five weeks of an AIP trial, illustrating compressed sales cycles and rapid trial-to-deal conversion.

**Financial Services and Insurance:**

AIG expects to double its five-year CAGR partly due to Palantir technology. Applications include advanced fraud detection and prevention, anti-money laundering (AML) and know your customer (KYC) compliance, risk management and capital adequacy, optimizing trading strategies, and underwriting and claims workflow automation via AIP agents.

The value proposition in financial services is increasingly about embedding agents into underwriting and claims workflows rather than just analytics dashboards. ROI is measured through direct reduction in financial losses, avoidance of regulatory fines, and improved operational efficiency.

### Partner Ecosystem and Channel Strategy

**Anduril Golden Dome Collaboration:**

Palantir and Anduril are collaborating on "Golden Dome" shield software, a defense initiative combining Palantir's data integration and AI capabilities with Anduril's autonomous systems expertise. This represents a notable partnership between two major defense technology companies. The collaboration extends Palantir's role in AI-driven defense infrastructure and demonstrates how partnerships can extend capabilities beyond what either company could provide alone.

**Bain and Company Consulting Partnership:**

Palantir expanded its consulting partnership with Bain and Company in early 2026. This type of alliance, where a major consulting firm embeds Palantir technology in its client engagements, serves as a channel multiplier for commercial adoption. Bain becomes a force multiplier, introducing Palantir to financial services and other corporate clients where consulting relationships provide trusted access.

**Rackspace Partnership:**

Palantir entered into a partnership with Rackspace, appearing to be a cloud infrastructure or hosting alliance that could facilitate enterprise deployments. This partnership addresses deployment and hosting considerations for enterprise customers.

**Ondas ISR Missions Partnership:**

Palantir partnered with Ondas Inc. for intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance (ISR) missions. This extends Palantir's role in defense-adjacent operational technology.

**Polymarket and TWG AI Sports Integrity:**

Palantir is involved in a partnership with Polymarket and TWG AI related to sports integrity. This represents a new vertical for Palantir's technology application, demonstrating the breadth of use cases beyond traditional government and industrial markets.

**FedStart Accreditation-as-a-Service:**

FedStart, Palantir's offering that provides accreditation-as-a-service for sensitive US government workloads, differentiates against hyperscalers who lack Palantir's deep government security accreditations. This offering addresses compliance and security barriers for companies doing business with the US government.

### Customer Concentration and Retention Metrics

**Government vs. Commercial Revenue Split:**

Palantir's revenue has historically been weighted toward government customers, providing stable, long-term contracts with high renewal rates. However, commercial revenue has accelerated with the AIP product launch. Specific percentage breakdowns by quarter are not available in the current research but represent a key metric for understanding customer concentration risk.

**Net Dollar Retention (NDR):**

Net dollar retention rates signal strong expansion within existing accounts. While specific NDR figures are not disclosed in available research, the company reports strong NDR as evidence that customers expand usage over time. This metric is critical for assessing Palantir's ability to grow within existing accounts versus relying on new customer acquisition.

**Customer Concentration Risk:**

Top 10 customer revenue as a percentage of total revenue is a key metric for assessing concentration risk. This data point is not available in current research but represents an important gap. High concentration would indicate dependence on a small number of large contracts, while broader distribution would indicate healthier diversification.

**Remaining Deal Value:**

Remaining deal value nearly tripled, indicating strong backlog and revenue visibility. This metric demonstrates the value of contracted but not yet recognized revenue, providing insight into future revenue trajectory.

### Sales Motion and Go-to-Market Evolution

**From Forward Deployed Engineers to Boot Camps:**

Palantir's traditional sales model relied heavily on Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs) who worked onsite at customer locations for extended periods. This model produced deep integration but was resource-intensive and slow to scale. The AIP boot camp model represents a fundamental shift: free workshops where customer teams build working agents themselves in days. This accelerates time to first value and reduces Palantir's cost of customer acquisition.

**Partner-Led Motion:**

The expansion of partnerships with Bain and Company, Rackspace, and other channel partners represents a new lever for growth. Rather than all customer relationships being direct, Palantir is building a partner ecosystem that multiplies its sales reach. This is particularly important in financial services and other verticals where consulting firms have trusted relationships.

**Direct Listing to Enterprise Adoption:**

Palantir's 2020 direct listing rather than traditional IPO marked its transition from venture-backed startup to public company. This event increased visibility and accessibility for enterprise customers who may have procurement policies favoring public vendors.

### Churn, Failures, and Reductions

**BP Scale-Back:**

BP notably scaled back its Palantir usage, representing a rare public example of customer disengagement. This reduction indicates that perceived long-term value and cost considerations can lead customers to reduce or eliminate Palantir deployments. Understanding why BP scaled back (cost vs. value, internal build vs. buy, competitive solutions) would provide important context for retention risk.

**NHS Front-Line Friction:**

Despite impressive macro-level metrics (80,000 additional procedures, hundreds of thousands of waiting list removals), Freedom of Information requests and campaign groups such as "No Palantir in the NHS" have collected feedback from staff describing issues including limited adoption, poor usability, restricted data access, technical barriers for analysts, and significant staff time spent wrestling with the system. This illustrates that even successful deployments can experience significant front-line friction that threatens long-term stickiness.

**POC Theatre and Agent Sprawl Risk:**

With hundreds of boot camps per quarter, only a minority convert to meaningful revenue. The risk is "POC theatre," where pilots never reach production or are not integrated into core workflows, leading to fatigue and disappointment despite strong demonstrations. This represents a churn risk at the pilot-to-production transition.

### Competitive Positioning Among Customers

**Ontology Moat and Category of One:**

UBS published research stating that Palantir's "ontology moat proves itself." Multiple analysts have applied "Category of One" framing to Palantir, arguing it defies traditional software categorisation. This positioning influences how customers perceive Palantir relative to alternatives.

**vs. Hyperscalers:**

Amazon Web Services, Microsoft Azure, and Google Cloud offer competing data and AI platforms. However, FedStart and Palantir's deep government security accreditations provide differentiation in sensitive government workloads. The ontology-first approach also differs from hyperscaler infrastructure-first approaches.

**vs. Specialized Competitors:**

Knox, a startup, raised $6.5 million specifically to compete in the federal compliance space. This signals continued entrepreneurial interest in the government technology market that Palantir dominates. While small today, specialized competitors may emerge in specific verticals.

**vs. In-House Builds:**

Some customers may choose to build capabilities internally rather than buy Palantir, particularly large technology companies with strong engineering teams. BP's scale-back may reflect this dynamic.
