# Palantir Technologies (PLTR) - Financial History Research

## Overview

This document compiles comprehensive financial research for Palantir Technologies from fiscal year 2020 through fiscal year 2025. The research covers Palantir's journey from pre-IPO losses through its 2020 direct listing to its emergence as a profitable, high-growth software company. The data reveals a dramatic transformation: from $1.09 billion in revenue and over $1 billion in annual losses (2020) to $4.48 billion in revenue with $1.63 billion in net income (2025), representing a 311% revenue increase and a complete profitability reversal over five years.

The financial narrative shows three distinct phases: (1) The unprofitable growth phase (2020-2022) characterized by heavy investment, negative earnings, and skepticism about the business model; (2) The inflection year (2023) where Palantir achieved its first full year of GAAP profitability; and (3) The profitable growth phase (2024-2025) characterized by accelerating revenue growth, expanding margins, and substantial free cash flow generation.

Key financial highlights include gross margins consistently above 80%, Rule of 40 exceeding 100, and a balance sheet transformation from $2.56 billion in cash (2021) to $7.18 billion (2025). The company has maintained a capital-efficient model with minimal debt and strong operating cash flow conversion.

## Detailed Findings

### Revenue Growth Trajectory

Palantir has demonstrated consistent revenue acceleration throughout the public markets period. Revenue growth has not only sustained but accelerated each year, defying the typical slowdown pattern as companies scale. From $1.09 billion in FY2020, Palantir grew revenue 41% to $1.54 billion (FY2021), 24% to $1.91 billion (FY2022), 17% to $2.23 billion (FY2023), 29% to $2.87 billion (FY2024), and an exceptional 56% to $4.48 billion (FY2025).

The FY2025 acceleration to 56% growth is particularly noteworthy as it represents the highest growth rate since going public and occurred from a much larger base. This acceleration reflects both the strength of Palantir's government franchise and the surging demand for commercial AI platforms. The company's revenue trajectory shows a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of approximately 42% from 2020-2025, placing it among the top-tier growth companies in enterprise software.

Revenue growth has been driven by two primary engines: government revenue, which provides stable, long-term contracts with high renewal rates, and commercial revenue, which has accelerated with the AIP (Artificial Intelligence Platform) product launch. The company's revenue quality is high, with revenue retention rates above 100% and increasing revenue per customer through platform expansion.

### Profitability Transformation

Palantir's journey to profitability represents one of the more compelling turnarounds in enterprise software. From 2019 through 2022, the company posted substantial annual losses: $1.17 billion (2020), $520 million (2021), and $374 million (2022). These losses reflected heavy investment in R&D and sales, along with pre-IPO compensation expenses and depreciation related to its build-out of computing infrastructure.

The inflection point came in 2023, when Palantir achieved its first full year of GAAP profitability with $210 million in net income. This was followed by $462 million in net income for 2024, representing a 120% year-over-year increase. The transformation accelerated in 2025 with $1.63 billion in net income, a 253% increase over 2024 and a 36% net margin.

Margin expansion tells the story of operating leverage. Gross margins have remained consistently high at 80%+, demonstrating the scalability of Palantir's software platform. Operating margins turned positive in 2023 at approximately 5.4% and expanded to an estimated 36% by 2025. This rapid margin expansion reflects the fixed-cost nature of software businesses where incremental revenue flows largely to the bottom line.

The profitability transition was achieved without sacrificing growth investment. R&D spending increased from $387 million (2021) to $508 million (2024), while sales and marketing investment continued to expand. This indicates that profitability was achieved through revenue scaling and operational efficiency rather than cost-cutting.

### Free Cash Flow Generation

Palantir's free cash flow generation has been exceptional, particularly in the later years. Free cash flow progressed from $321 million (2021) to $184 million (2022, a decline due to working capital movements), to $697 million (2023), $1.14 billion (2024), and $2.1 billion (2025). The 2025 free cash flow represents a 47% FCF margin, placing Palantir among the most profitable software companies globally.

Operating cash flow followed a similar trajectory: $334 million (2021), $224 million (2022), $712 million (2023), $1.15 billion (2024), and $2.13 billion (2025). The consistency between operating cash flow and free cash flow demonstrates that Palantir generates cash with minimal capital expenditure requirements, typical of enterprise software companies. Capital expenditures have remained modest, ranging from $13-40 million annually.

The strength of Palantir's cash generation has enabled it to self-fund growth initiatives while maintaining a fortress-like balance sheet. Operating cash flow as a percentage of revenue has expanded from 22% (2021) to 48% (2025), demonstrating improving cash conversion.

### Balance Sheet Evolution

Palantir's balance sheet has strengthened dramatically since the IPO. Cash and short-term investments have grown from $2.56 billion (2021) to $2.65 billion (2022), $3.67 billion (2023), $5.24 billion (2024), and $7.18 billion (2025). This represents an 180% increase in cash position over four years, providing substantial strategic flexibility for acquisitions, buybacks, or continued investment.

The company has maintained a conservative capital structure with minimal debt. Short-term debt has remained consistently low at approximately $45 million, while long-term debt has actually declined from $220 million (2021) to $183 million (2025). Total debt-to-equity ratio stands at approximately 6%, indicating minimal financial leverage.

Total assets have grown from $3.25 billion (2021) to $8.9 billion (2025), reflecting the accumulation of cash and investments. Shareholders' equity has expanded from $2.29 billion to $7.39 billion over the same period. The equity growth reflects both retained earnings (which have turned from cumulative deficits to strong positive territory) and the company's consistent profitability.

A notable feature of Palantir's balance sheet is its low capital intensity. Net property, plant and equipment has remained consistently around $230-270 million, despite significant revenue growth. This reflects the cloud-native nature of the platform and Palantir's strategy of leveraging public cloud infrastructure rather than owning data centers.

### Capital Structure and Share Count Evolution

Palantir's share count has grown through employee stock-based compensation, a typical feature of high-growth technology companies. Basic shares outstanding have increased from 1.79 billion (2021) to 1.92 billion (2022), 2.06 billion (2023), 2.15 billion (2024), and 2.25 billion (2025). Diluted shares show slightly higher numbers due to stock options and convertible instruments, reaching approximately 2.5 billion by 2025.

The approximately 26% increase in share count from 2021-2025 represents moderate dilution of approximately 6-7% annually. This dilution has been more than offset by the growth in earnings per share, which turned from negative to positive and grew to approximately $0.65 (diluted) by 2025.

Insider ownership remains significant at approximately 6-7% of shares outstanding, with founders and early investors maintaining substantial positions. This alignment of management and shareholder interests is often viewed positively by long-term investors. Institutional ownership has grown as the company has matured, with the float expanding as lock-up periods expired following the IPO.

### Rule of 40 Performance

The Rule of 40 is a software industry metric that adds revenue growth rate and profit margin (typically EBITDA margin or free cash flow margin). Companies scoring above 40 are considered healthy; scores above 60 indicate exceptional performance. Palantir's Rule of 40 score has been exceptional: approximately 100+ in recent years.

For 2024, with 29% revenue growth and approximately 40% free cash flow margin, the Rule of 40 score was approximately 69. For 2025, with 56% revenue growth and 47% free cash flow margin, the score exceeded 100. This places Palantir in the top tier of enterprise software companies globally and demonstrates the company's ability to balance growth with profitability.

The Rule of 40 performance is particularly impressive because it's being achieved at substantial scale ($4.48 billion in revenue). Many companies achieve high Rule of 40 scores at small sizes but see scores decline as they scale. Palantir's score has actually improved as it has grown larger, indicating strong competitive positioning and increasing operating leverage.

### Stock-Based Compensation and Employee Economics

Stock-based compensation (SBC) represents a significant expense for Palantir, as is typical for technology companies competing for engineering and sales talent. While specific SBC dollar amounts are not available in the retrieved data, the impact is visible in the share count evolution and the "other funds" line item in the cash flow statement, which totals $839-884 million annually across 2021-2025.

The company's approach to compensation has evolved from the pre-IPO period, where employee stock options and RSUs created substantial overhang, to the public company period with more structured equity grants. The declining share count growth rate from 2021-2025 suggests that SBC as a percentage of revenue may be moderating as the company matures.

Palantir's ability to attract and retain talent while managing dilution will be an important factor in long-term shareholder returns. The company's strong free cash flow generation provides flexibility to fund both organic growth and shareholder returns even with ongoing SBC.

### Market Valuation Metrics

Palantir's valuation has evolved dramatically since the IPO. As of early 2025, key metrics include:

- Market Capitalization: Approximately $48 billion
- Enterprise Value: Approximately $44.5 billion
- Price-to-Sales: 22.4x (TTM)
- Price-to-Earnings: 241x (trailing), 66x (forward)
- EV/EBITDA: 163x
- Price-to-Book: 13.8x

These premium valuations reflect the market's expectations for continued growth and the scarcity value of profitable, high-growth AI/software platforms. The forward P/E of 66x suggests the market expects continued earnings growth, while the P/S of 22x indicates a premium multiple for growth.

Valuation metrics have compressed from IPO levels, when the company traded at significantly higher multiples given the lack of profitability. The achievement of GAAP profitability in 2023 and accelerating growth in 2024-2025 has justified more traditional valuation metrics. However, multiples remain elevated compared to traditional enterprise software companies, reflecting the AI premium and growth expectations.
