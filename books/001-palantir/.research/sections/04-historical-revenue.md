## From $595 million to $4.5 billion: the revenue journey

Palantir's revenue trajectory from fiscal year 2018 through fiscal year 2025 represents one of the more remarkable growth stories in enterprise software. The journey from $595 million in annual revenue to approximately $4.48 billion spans seven years and encompasses three distinct phases: steady pre-IPO expansion, post-listing deceleration as the company navigated public market scrutiny, and a dramatic post-2023 re-acceleration driven by artificial intelligence platform adoption. What makes this trajectory noteworthy is not merely the compound growth rate but the way growth accelerated rather than decelerated as the revenue base expanded. In 2025, Palantir achieved 56 percent year-over-year growth from a multi-billion dollar base, its highest growth rate since going public and a figure that defies the typical scaling dynamics where growth slows as companies mature.

The pre-IPO period established the foundation. In fiscal year 2018, Palantir generated approximately $595 million in revenue. The following year, 2019, revenue reached $743 million, representing 25 percent year-over-year growth. This period was characterized by deep government contract penetration and the early stages of commercial expansion. The company was still private, operating with the luxury of focusing on long-term contract development rather than quarterly earnings guidance. Government revenue dominated this period, with the United States military and intelligence community providing the bedrock of recurring revenue through multi-year contracts for Gotham deployments.

Fiscal year 2020 marked a significant inflection point. Revenue reached $1.09 billion, representing 47 percent year-over-year growth and pushing Palantir past the billion-dollar annual revenue milestone. This was the year Palantir completed its direct listing on the New York Stock Exchange in September 2020, pricing at $7.25 per share. The pandemic year proved unexpectedly favorable for Palantir as government agencies sought data integration capabilities for crisis response and commercial enterprises accelerated digital transformation initiatives. However, this period also marked the beginning of investor scrutiny around profitability, as Palantir posted a net loss of approximately $1.17 billion in 2020, largely driven by stock-based compensation expenses and pre-IPO compensation adjustments.

The post-IPO period began with continued momentum. Fiscal year 2021 delivered $1.54 billion in revenue, 41 percent growth over 2020. This growth rate, while robust, represented a deceleration from the prior year's 47 percent, a pattern that would concern investors in subsequent years. Government revenue continued to provide stability, with large defense and intelligence agency renewals underpinning the top line. Commercial revenue began gaining traction but faced headwinds as Palantir rebuilt its direct sales force and adjusted to public company operating cadences. The company remained unprofitable in 2021, posting a net loss of approximately $520 million, though this represented substantial improvement from the 2020 loss figure.

Fiscal year 2022 marked the low point of Palantir's growth trajectory. Revenue reached $1.91 billion, representing 24 percent year-over-year growth. This further deceleration from 41 percent to 24 percent raised questions about whether Palantir could sustain expansion as its government contracts matured and commercial execution proved challenging. The company posted a net loss of approximately $374 million, its third consecutive year of substantial losses. Government revenue grew but at a measured pace, while commercial revenue faced macroeconomic headwinds including rising interest rates and enterprise caution on large software commitments. The narrative in financial media and analyst reports during this period focused on whether Palantir could achieve profitability and whether growth would continue slowing into the mid-teens.

Fiscal year 2023 proved to be the inflection point that reversed this narrative. Revenue reached $2.23 billion, representing 17 percent year-over-year growth. While this represented yet another deceleration from the prior year's 24 percent, the more significant story was on the profitability line. Palantir achieved its first full year of GAAP profitability with $210 million in net income. This milestone validated the company's long-stated argument that heavy upfront investment would yield operating leverage at scale. More importantly, 2023 marked the launch of the Artificial Intelligence Platform (AIP) in April, a product introduction that would catalyze the growth re-acceleration to follow. The seeds planted during 2023, through AIP boot camps and early commercial deployments, set the stage for the dramatic expansion that followed.

Fiscal year 2024 demonstrated the first fruits of the AIP strategy. Revenue reached $2.87 billion, representing 29 percent year-over-year growth. This marked the first acceleration in annual growth since going public, reversing the multi-year deceleration pattern from 47 percent to 41 percent to 24 percent to 17 percent. Net income reached $462 million, a 120 percent increase over 2023 and representing a 16 percent net margin. The government segment generated approximately $1.6 billion, or 55 percent of total revenue, while commercial reached approximately $1.3 billion, or 45 percent. The United States accounted for approximately two-thirds of total revenue, with international commercial proving more challenging than the domestic market. The Rule of 40 metric, which combines revenue growth percentage and profit margin percentage, reached approximately 69, placing Palantir among the top tier of enterprise software companies.

Fiscal year 2025 delivered the full flowering of the AIP-driven transformation. Revenue reached approximately $4.48 billion, representing 56 percent year-over-year growth. This marked the highest growth rate since going public and occurred from a much larger base, defying conventional scaling dynamics. Net income reached $1.63 billion, a 253 percent increase over 2024 and representing a 36 percent net margin. The Rule of 40 exceeded 100, a level typically achieved only by the most exceptional software franchises. Free cash flow reached approximately $2.1 billion, representing a 47 percent margin. The company raised revenue guidance three times during the year, ultimately exceeding the raised range of $4.396 to $4.40 billion. US commercial revenue grew more than 100 percent year over year, reaching approximately $1.433 billion and representing roughly 32 percent of total revenue. Government revenue remained robust at approximately $2.5 billion, now representing 55 percent of total, but commercial was growing faster and narrowing the gap.

The quarterly progression from early 2023 through late 2025 reveals the accelerating momentum. In the first quarter of 2023, Palantir generated approximately $491 million in revenue with modest year-over-year growth. By the fourth quarter of 2023, quarterly revenue reached approximately $608 million. The April 2023 AIP launch began showing results in the second half of the year. In the first quarter of 2024, revenue reached approximately $634 million. The second quarter of 2024 delivered approximately $678 million. The third quarter of 2024 reached approximately $726 million. The fourth quarter of 2024 achieved approximately $826 million, with GAAP net income of $79 million representing a 10 percent margin and US revenue up 52 percent year over year.

The acceleration intensified in 2025. The first quarter of 2025 delivered approximately $875 million in revenue. The second quarter of 2025 marked a milestone with approximately $1.0 billion in quarterly revenue, the first time Palantir achieved a billion-dollar revenue quarter. GAAP operating margin reached approximately 27 percent, adjusted operating margin approximately 46 percent, and Rule of 40 around 94. GAAP earnings per share reached $0.13 with record net income. The third quarter of 2025 demonstrated further acceleration with approximately $1.18 billion in quarterly revenue. GAAP operating margin reached approximately 33 percent, GAAP net income margin approximately 40 percent, and Rule of 40 above 110. GAAP earnings per share reached approximately $0.18 to $0.21, with GAAP net income of approximately $476 million.

The government versus commercial split evolution tells a strategic story. In the early years, government revenue dominated, often exceeding 60 percent of total revenue. This reflected Palantir's origins in defense and intelligence work, with Gotham deployments across the Department of Defense, intelligence agencies, and civilian agencies like Health and Human Services and the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. Government revenue provided stability through long-term contracts, high security clearances, and mission-critical deployments that created significant switching costs. However, government growth was inherently constrained by budget cycles, procurement processes, and the finite nature of defense spending.

The strategic pivot toward commercial revenue gained urgency as Palantir prepared for its public listing and investors sought growth beyond the government franchise. Commercial revenue represented the primary growth vector but proved challenging to scale initially. The AIP boot camp model, intensive five-day workshops where prospective customers brought their own data and business problems to build working AI workflows in days rather than months, proved to be the breakthrough. This product-led motion dramatically shortened sales cycles and demonstrated tangible value before contract signature. US commercial revenue grew 93 percent year over year in the second quarter of 2025 to $306 million, then 121 percent year over year in the third quarter of 2025 to approximately $397 million, representing around three-quarters of total commercial revenue that quarter.

International commercial revenue has faced relative headwinds. While the United States commercial segment has delivered triple-digit growth, international commercial has lagged. This reflects several factors including more stringent data sovereignty regulations in Europe, different procurement cultures in Asia-Pacific, and the concentration of Palantir's go-to-market resources in the domestic market. The United States now accounts for approximately two-thirds of total revenue, with international representing one-third. Management has indicated international commercial expansion as a priority, but the execution has proven more challenging than the domestic surge.

The path to profitability represents a parallel narrative to the revenue journey. From 2019 through 2022, Palantir posted substantial annual losses of approximately $1.17 billion in 2020, $520 million in 2021, and $374 million in 2022. These losses reflected heavy investment in research and development, sales and marketing, and the pre-IPO compensation structure that included significant stock-based compensation. The inflection point came in 2023 with $210 million in net income, followed by $462 million in 2024, then $1.63 billion in 2025. Margin expansion tells the story of operating leverage. Operating margins turned positive in 2023 at approximately 5.4 percent, expanded to approximately 10.8 percent in 2024, and reached approximately 36 percent in 2025.

Free cash flow generation has paralleled the profitability transformation. After declining from $321 million in 2021 to $184 million in 2022 due to working capital movements, free cash flow reached $697 million in 2023, $1.14 billion in 2024, and $2.1 billion in 2025. The 2025 free cash flow represents a 47 percent margin, placing Palantir among the most profitable software companies globally. Operating cash flow followed a similar trajectory, reaching $2.13 billion in 2025 with operating cash flow as a percentage of revenue expanding from 22 percent in 2021 to 48 percent in 2025.

Revenue guidance evolution reflects management's increasing confidence. In 2023, guidance was conservative as the AIP product was just launching. By 2024, guidance began reflecting the momentum, with raises throughout the year. In 2025, Palantir raised revenue guidance three times, initially providing a range that was ultimately exceeded. The pattern of guidance raises and beats contributed to investor confidence and multiple expansion, with the stock reaching an all-time high of approximately $207.52 in late 2025 before declining roughly 26 percent to approximately $128 by early 2026 amid broader AI sector volatility.

The balance sheet has strengthened alongside revenue growth. Cash and short-term investments grew from $2.56 billion in 2021 to $7.18 billion in 2025, providing substantial strategic flexibility. Total debt remained minimal at approximately $229 million. The company maintained a conservative capital structure with stock repurchases in the tens of millions per quarter during 2024 and 2025, while reinvesting the vast majority of excess cash into short-dated US Treasuries. This fortress balance sheet enabled self-funding of growth initiatives while maintaining optionality for acquisitions or more aggressive capital returns.

Looking at the full trajectory, the compound annual growth rate from 2020 through 2025 approximates 42 percent, placing Palantir among the top-tier growth companies in enterprise software. What distinguishes this growth story is the re-acceleration rather than sustained deceleration. The 17 percent growth in 2023 represented a low point, followed by 29 percent in 2024 and 56 percent in 2025. This pattern of accelerating growth from an expanding base is rare in enterprise software and reflects the timing of the AI wave coinciding with Palantir's product launch and distribution model maturity.

The revenue journey reveals three strategic insights. First, the government franchise provided a stable foundation that funded commercial expansion without requiring external capital. Second, the AIP product launch coincided with enterprise AI adoption readiness, creating a timing advantage. Third, the boot camp go-to-market model proved highly effective at converting AI interest into deployed contracts, particularly in the United States market. The result is a company that transformed from unprofitable growth to profitable growth while simultaneously accelerating revenue expansion from a multi-billion dollar base.
