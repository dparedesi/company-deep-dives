# NVIDIA History, Founding, and Leadership Research

## Founding Story

### The Denny's Meeting (1993)

NVIDIA was founded on April 5, 1993, by three computer scientists who agreed to start the company at a Denny's roadside diner on Berryessa Road in East San Jose, California. The founders were:

- **Jensen Huang** (Jen-Hsun Huang): Previously a director at LSI Logic and microprocessor designer at AMD
- **Chris Malachowsky**: An engineer at Sun Microsystems
- **Curtis Priem**: A senior staff engineer and graphics chip designer at IBM and Sun Microsystems

The Denny's location was chosen because it was quieter than home and had cheap coffee. Huang had previous experience working at Denny's in Oregon in the 1980s as a dishwasher and busboy.

### Initial Capital and Funding

The company began with $40,000 in capital from the founders and subsequently received $20 million in venture capital funding from Sequoia Capital, Sutter Hill Ventures, and others. This early funding gave the company the financial support and market credibility needed to survive its difficult first years.

### Naming the Company

The founders initially considered "NVision" but discovered it was already taken by a toilet paper manufacturer. They settled on "NVIDIA," derived from "invidia," the Latin word for "envy." The "NV" portion originally stood for "next version," referring to next-generation graphics chips.

## Early Struggles and Near-Death Experience

### The NV1 Failure

NVIDIA's first product, the NV1, was designed to process quadrilateral primitives rather than the industry-standard triangle primitives. When Microsoft introduced DirectX and announced it would exclusively support triangles, the NV1 failed to gain market traction.

### Sega Dreamcast Crisis and Turnaround

NVIDIA had entered a partnership with Sega to supply graphics chips for the Dreamcast console, but the company's technology was lagging behind competitors. In a pivotal moment, Sega president Shoichiro Irimajiri personally informed Huang that Sega had chosen another vendor. However, believing in NVIDIA's potential, Irimajiri persuaded Sega's management to invest $5 million in the company. Huang later reflected that this funding was all that kept NVIDIA afloat, giving them "six months to live."

### The RIVA 128 Pivot and Survival

In 1996, Huang laid off more than half of NVIDIA's employees, reducing headcount from 100 to 40, and focused the company's remaining resources on developing the RIVA 128, a graphics accelerator optimized for triangle primitives. By the time the RIVA 128 was released in August 1997, NVIDIA had only enough money left for one month's payroll. The sense of impending failure gave rise to NVIDIA's unofficial company motto: "Our company is thirty days from going out of business." Huang opened internal presentations with these words for many years.

The RIVA 128 was a success, selling about a million units within four months, and provided revenue to fund the next generation of products.

### IPO and Success

NVIDIA went public on January 22, 1999. In late 1999, the company released the GeForce 256 (NV10), its first product expressly marketed as a GPU, which introduced onboard transformation and lighting (T&L) to consumer-level 3D hardware. The success of these products won NVIDIA the contract to develop graphics hardware for Microsoft's Xbox, which included a $200 million advance.

## Key Milestones and Pivots

### 2006: CUDA Platform Launch

NVIDIA released its Compute Unified Device Architecture (CUDA) platform in 2006, a major innovation in GPU-based parallel computing technology. The platform enabled GPUs to run massively parallel programs for a broad range of compute-intensive applications. This investment, which cost over a billion dollars, initially met with Wall Street skepticism but would prove crucial for the AI revolution.

### 2012: AlexNet and AI Breakthrough

In 2012, Alex Krizhevsky's neural network, AlexNet, was trained on two GeForce graphics cards using NVIDIA's CUDA platform. AlexNet won the ImageNet visual recognition competition by such a large margin that organizers initially suspected cheating. This became known as the "Big Bang" moment for deep learning, demonstrating that GPUs could train neural networks up to 100 times faster than CPUs. AlexNet has been cited more than 100,000 times, making it one of the most important papers in computer science history.

### 2013: Strategic Pivot to AI

After the success of AlexNet, Huang sent an email on a Friday evening declaring that "everything is going to deep learning" and that NVIDIA was "no longer a graphics company." By Monday morning, the company had repositioned itself as an AI company. Huang approached his lead researcher with a thought experiment about marching all 8,000 employees into the parking lot and letting him select anyone for his AI team.

### Major Acquisitions

- **2000**: Acquired intellectual assets of 3dfx Interactive (a pioneer in consumer 3D graphics)
- **2011**: Acquired Icera, a baseband chip maker, for $367 million
- **2019**: Acquired Mellanox Technologies for $6.9 billion, expanding footprint in high-performance computing
- **2020**: Acquired Cumulus Networks, absorbed into networking business unit

### Failed Arm Acquisition

In September 2020, NVIDIA announced it would buy Arm Ltd. from SoftBank for $40 billion. The deal faced significant regulatory scrutiny globally and was ultimately called off in February 2022 due to "significant regulatory challenges." This would have been the largest semiconductor acquisition in history.

## Jensen Huang: Personal Story and Leadership

### Early Life

Jensen Huang (Jen-Hsun Huang) was born in Tainan, Taiwan, on February 17, 1963. His father was a chemical engineer and his mother taught grade school. When he was five, the family moved to Thailand. Due to the Vietnam War, his parents decided not to settle permanently in Thailand.

### Journey to America

At age nine, Huang and his brother were sent as unaccompanied minors to live with an uncle in Tacoma, Washington. They were sent to the Oneida Baptist Institute in rural Kentucky, which Huang's uncle mistakenly believed was a prestigious boarding school. In reality, it was a religious reform school for "difficult" children. Huang was forced to clean toilets daily, faced relentless bullying, and was subjected to ethnic slurs and knife threats.

### Resilience and Recovery

After two years, Huang's parents arrived in the United States, discovered the situation, and pulled their children out. The family settled in the suburbs of Portland, Oregon. Huang attended Aloha High School, where he played competitive table tennis and earned a national ranking. He graduated in 1981 and attended Oregon State University.

### Education and Early Career

At Oregon State, Huang met Lori Mills, a fellow engineering student who would become his wife. He graduated with a bachelor's degree in electrical engineering in 1984. He worked at Advanced Micro Devices (AMD) for about a year before joining LSI Logic, where he rose through the ranks to become director of a division. While working, he earned a master's degree in electrical engineering from Stanford University.

### Leadership Style and Philosophy

Huang's leadership is characterized by:

- **"Speed of light" thinking**: Considering the absolute fastest a task could be accomplished, then working backward
- **"Zero-billion-dollar market" pursuit**: Exploring products without obvious competitors or customers (like CUDA initially)
- **"Failure must be shared"**: Employees present failures to audiences as a learning ritual
- **Intense communication**: Writes hundreds of brief emails daily, often compared to haiku
- **No fixed hierarchy**: Employees submit weekly lists of their five most important tasks
- **Direct interrogation**: Known to stop at junior employees' desks and quiz them deeply on their work

Huang is known for his leather jacket attire at keynotes and public appearances. When NVIDIA's stock hit $100 per share, he got a tattoo of the company logo on his left shoulder.

### Personal Life

Huang married Lori Mills in a relationship that began when he persistently asked her to do homework with him at Oregon State. They have two children who both eventually pursued careers at NVIDIA after working in the hospitality industry. In 2007, they founded the Jen-Hsun and Lori Huang Foundation for philanthropic giving.

## Company Culture and "30 Days from Bankruptcy" Mentality

### The Unofficial Motto

The phrase "Our company is thirty days from going out of business" became NVIDIA's unofficial corporate motto, born from the company's near-death experience in 1997. Huang began internal presentations with these words for many years, instilling a sense of urgency and humility even as the company grew into a trillion-dollar giant.

### Employee Demographics and Environment

NVIDIA's headquarters in Santa Clara features two enormous triangle-shaped buildings called "spaceships" by employees. The workforce is diverse: approximately one-third South Asian, one-third East Asian, and one-third white, though overwhelmingly male.

Post-COVID, only about one-third of the workforce shows up on any given day. The company has been ranked as one of America's best places to work, with bars at the top of each building and regular happy hours.

### Tracking and Efficiency

The company uses AI and video cameras to track employees throughout the day. If an employee eats a meal at a conference table, the AI can dispatch a janitor within an hour to clean up.

## HQ Locations and Global Presence

### Headquarters

- **Primary HQ**: Santa Clara, California (two triangle-shaped buildings called Endeavor and Voyager)
- **Historical HQ**: Sunnyvale, California (original headquarters, still in use)

### Major International Locations

- **Israel**: Major R&D center with over 4,500 employees (about 15% of global workforce), including acquisition of Mellanox Technologies. NVIDIA announced plans for a major new R&D campus in Kiryat Tivon, projected to employ over 10,000 people.
- **Armenia**: Research center in Yerevan (announced 2022)
- **Multiple offices**: Globally distributed workforce

## Employee Count and Growth

NVIDIA has experienced dramatic employee growth:
- 2016: 9,227 employees
- 2020: 13,775 employees
- 2023: 26,000 employees
- 2025: 36,000 employees
- 2026: 42,000 employees

In 2023, half of NVIDIA employees earned over $228,000. The company has been described as the "hot employer" in Silicon Valley due to interesting work and high pay during a period of tech industry downsizing.

## The Leather Jacket Keynote Tradition

Jensen Huang's leather jacket has become his signature look at NVIDIA keynotes and public appearances. The black leather jacket, black jeans, and black shoes ensemble is widely imitated and has been featured in the New York Times Style section. At public events, Huang is known for his sarcastic, self-deprecating humor and high-energy presentations, despite claiming to hate public speaking and being quite introverted.

## "Huang's Law"

At the 2018 GPU Technology Conference, Huang remarked that NVIDIA's GPUs were 25 times faster than they had been five years prior. The observation that GPU performance was more than doubling every two years (outpacing Moore's Law) was dubbed "Huang's Law" by the press.

## Company Mission and Vision Statements

While specific mission statements were not found in the research, Huang has articulated a vision of:
- Democratizing supercomputing through CUDA
- Using GPUs to solve computational problems that eluded general-purpose computing
- Building the "industrial metaverse" through Omniverse

The company describes itself as "the AI computing company" and positions its GPUs as the engine of the new industrial revolution.
