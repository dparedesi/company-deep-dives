## F4: Balance sheet transformation: $25.0 billion bond sale, receivables spike to $63.1 billion, and NVIDIA as lender of last resort to its customers
**Type:** risk
**Chapters:** 5, 8, 4
**Confidence:** high
**Date:** 2026-08-26
**Materiality:** thesis-changing
**Supersedes:** April edition's debt-free, self-funding cash-machine characterization

NVIDIA priced $25.0 billion of senior notes on June 15, 2026, its first bond sale since 2021 and largest ever (seven tranches out to 2056, $85 billion of orders, upsized from $20 billion), and Q2 operating cash flow fell 52% sequentially to $24.1 billion even as profit rose, because receivables jumped from $40.7 billion to $63.1 billion (DSO from 45 to 60 days) on extended payment terms to investment-grade customers. The Q2 disclosures also carried $279 billion of supply and capacity commitments, $29 billion of cloud commitments, a guarantee package capped at $105 billion for a single data center campus, minimum revenue guarantees for NeoClouds, and financing partnerships with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR meant to mobilize over $500 billion of third-party capital. The April edition's portrait of a debt-free cash machine that never finances its customers needs a full rewrite: NVIDIA is increasingly the balance sheet behind its own buyers.
