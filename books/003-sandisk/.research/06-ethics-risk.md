# SanDisk Corporation: Ethics, Controversies, and Risks Research

Research date: 2026-04-11

---

## Overview

SanDisk Corporation's ethical profile presents a mix of strong supply chain responsibility and significant product-related controversies that occurred primarily under Western Digital's ownership (2016-2025). The company demonstrated leadership in conflict minerals sourcing, ranking 3rd of 24 consumer electronics companies in the Enough Project's 2012 survey. However, the Western Digital acquisition era brought multiple product controversies including misleading marketing about drive specifications, undisclosed use of inferior recording technology in NAS drives, security vulnerabilities in self-encrypting drives, and a major cyberattack exposing customer data. The company also faces structural risks inherent to the NAND flash industry: extreme commodity cyclicality, geopolitical exposure through Japanese manufacturing, massive capital intensity, and competitive pressure from Asian manufacturers with state support.

### 6. Product Quality Issues and Controversies

**Status:** MULTIPLE CONTROVERSIES, primarily under Western Digital ownership.

#### 6.1 WD Green Drives - Misleading RPM Claims
- In 2007, Western Digital advertised WD GP (Green Power) drives with rotational speed "between 7200 and 5400 rpm"
- This claim was technically correct but misleading: the drive actually spins at 5405 rpm with variable spin speed not implemented as advertised
- The marketing created consumer confusion about actual performance characteristics

#### 6.2 WD Red Drives - SMR Technology Controversy
- In May 2020, Western Digital was sued for using shingled magnetic recording (SMR) technology in WD Red NAS drives without explicit consumer disclosure
- SMR technology is unsuitable for RAID array applications commonly used with NAS drives
- Competitor Seagate stated SMR should not be used in NAS applications
- In June 2020, WD responded by introducing "Red Plus" branding for CMR (conventional magnetic recording) drives, while "WD Red" would primarily indicate SMR technology for lighter workloads

#### 6.3 My Book Live Data Deletion Incident
- In June 2021, users reported that discontinued My Book Live NAS drives (last manufactured in 2013) were remotely wiped
- WD advised users to disconnect these devices from the internet
- Incident highlighted security vulnerabilities in legacy connected storage products

#### 6.4 Storage Capacity Marketing
- Western Digital settled a class-action lawsuit in March 2006 over hard drive capacity labeling
- Drives were labeled using decimal (1,000 bytes per KB) while operating systems use binary (1,024 bytes per KB), creating perceived capacity shortfall
- WD offered $30 refunds in the form of backup software

**Sources:** Wikipedia - Western Digital

### 7. Security Vulnerabilities and Data Privacy

**Status:** MAJOR SECURITY INCIDENTS under Western Digital ownership.

#### 7.1 Self-Encrypting Hard Drive Flaws
- Western Digital external hard drives with encryption software (My Passport brand) reported to have severe data protection faults
- Security researchers found the encryption "easy to decrypt"
- Reports characterized the encryption as "useless" and "riddled with security flaws"

#### 7.2 2023 Cyberattack
- March 23, 2023: Western Digital experienced a cyberattack breaching company systems
- April 2, 2023: WD took services offline including My Cloud to assess the intrusion
- Attackers exfiltrated approximately **10 TB of data** including customer information
- Ransom demand: "minimum 8 figures" (at least $10 million)
- My Cloud service was restored after 10 days on April 13, 2023

**Sources:** Wikipedia - Western Digital

### 9. Acquisition Controversy

**Status:** The 2016 Western Digital acquisition of SanDisk had notable controversy.

**Key Details:**
- October 2015: WD announced intent to acquire SanDisk for **$19 billion**
- May 12, 2016: Acquisition completed at reduced valuation of **$16 billion**
- Reduction occurred after Chinese company **Unisplendour** withdrew from agreement to acquire 15% stake in WD
- Unisplendour's withdrawal followed regulatory scrutiny, likely related to CFIUS or Chinese government review

**Strategic Context:**
- Acquisition provided WD with NAND flash manufacturing capability through Toshiba joint venture
- Allowed diversification away from declining HDD market
- The 2025 spin-off suggests the integration may not have achieved strategic objectives

**Sources:** Wikipedia - SanDisk; Wikipedia - Western Digital

### 10. Commodity Cycle and Financial Risk

**Status:** STRUCTURAL RISK. SanDisk operates in an extremely cyclical industry.

**NAND Flash Cycle Characteristics:**
- Boom phases: Supply constraints drive price increases and strong profitability
- Bust phases: Oversupply leads to price collapse and industry losses
- Capital intensity: New fabrication facilities cost $10-20 billion
- Price erosion: Long-term trend of 25-35% annual cost-per-gigabyte decline
- Recent cycles: 2021-2022 shortage, 2023 severe downturn with 40-50% price declines

**Impact on SanDisk:**
- As a pure-play NAND manufacturer, SanDisk's profitability directly correlated to flash pricing
- The 2016 acquisition by WD provided balance sheet stability during downcycles
- The 2025 spin-off returns Sandisk to independent exposure to these cycles

**2025 Financial Context (Pre-Spin-Off):**
- Revenue: $7.355 billion
- Net Income: **-$1.64 billion** (loss)
- This loss illustrates continued industry volatility

**Sources:** Wikipedia - SanDisk; Wikipedia - Western Digital; Wikipedia - NAND flash memory

### 11. Technology Obsolescence Risk

**Status:** MODERATE RISK. NAND flash faces competition from emerging storage technologies.

**SanDisk's Position:**
- Pioneered flash storage but industry is maturing
- 3D NAND layer counts increasing (64-layer → 128-layer → 232+ layers)
- Transition from planar to 3D NAND required massive R&D investment

**Emerging Technologies (DATA GAPS on commercialization timeline):**
- CXL memory pooling
- DNA storage (experimental)
- Phase-change memory (PCM)
- MRAM/ReRAM (limited commercial adoption)

**Risk Assessment:** NAND flash remains dominant for most applications, but emerging technologies could disrupt specific use cases. SanDisk's 2025 spin-off suggests need for focused strategy to address these transitions.

**Sources:** Wikipedia - Flash memory; Inferred industry analysis

### 12. Geopolitical Risk

**Status:** SIGNIFICANT EXPOSURE through manufacturing and export controls.

**Manufacturing Concentration:**
- All NAND manufacturing occurs through joint venture in Yokkaichi, Japan (with Kioxia/Toshiba)
- Creates geopolitical exposure to Japan-specific risks

**Supply Chain Vulnerabilities:**
- June 2019: Power outage at Kioxia factory in Yokkaichi resulted in loss of **6-15 exabytes** of flash memory
- February 2022: Contamination issues at Yokkaichi and Kiakami factories affected **6.5 exabytes** of output
- These incidents demonstrate concentration risk

**China Export Controls:**
- US export controls on advanced semiconductor equipment to China (2022-2024) affect competitor YMTC
- While SanDisk/WD does not manufacture in China, geopolitical tensions affect overall market dynamics

**Acquisition-Related Geopolitics:**
- Unisplendour (Chinese company) withdrawal from WD stake acquisition in 2016 likely related to government review
- CFIUS or similar mechanisms influenced cross-border investment decisions

**Sources:** Wikipedia - Western Digital; Wikipedia - NAND flash memory

### 13. Competitive Risk

**Status:** HIGH COMPETITIVE INTENSITY in consolidated market.

**Market Structure (Q3 2025):**
- Samsung: 30% market share
- SK Hynix: 20% (expanded with 2022 Intel NAND acquisition)
- Kioxia: 14% (WD's joint venture partner)
- Micron: 13%
- YMTC (China): 13% (subject to US export controls)
- Western Digital: 11%

**Competitive Challenges:**
- Samsung: Vertically integrated, technology leader
- SK Hynix: Scale advantages through Intel acquisition
- YMTC: State-backed Chinese competitor (though constrained by export controls)
- Micron: Only other major US-based NAND manufacturer

**SanDisk's Position:**
- Differentiated through retail brand in consumer removable storage
- Joint venture with Kioxia provides manufacturing scale
- Patent portfolio provides licensing revenue
- But: Not a leader in mobile embedded flash (largest NAND consumption segment)

**Sources:** Wikipedia - NAND flash memory

## Strategic Assessment

### Ethical Strengths
1. **Supply Chain Leadership:** Strong conflict minerals sourcing ranking (3rd of 24 in 2012)
2. **Technology Recognition:** National Medal of Technology and Innovation for co-founder
3. **Product Innovation:** Pioneering role in flash storage development

### Ethical Concerns
1. **Product Marketing (WD Era):** Misleading RPM claims, undisclosed SMR technology
2. **Security Failures:** Self-encrypting drive vulnerabilities, major 2023 cyberattack
3. **Customer Impact:** My Book Live data deletion incident, class-action settlements

### Structural Risks
1. **Commodity Exposure:** Extreme NAND price cyclicality directly impacts profitability
2. **Manufacturing Concentration:** Single location (Yokkaichi, Japan) creates single-point-of-failure risk
3. **Competitive Pressure:** Market dominated by larger Asian manufacturers with scale advantages
4. **Capital Intensity:** $10-20 billion required for new fabrication facilities
5. **Geopolitical Sensitivity:** Export controls and cross-border investment reviews

### Post-2025 Spinoff Risk Profile
The February 2025 spin-off returns Sandisk to independent status, recreating the risk profile that necessitated the 2016 WD acquisition:
- Exposure to NAND commodity cycles without HDD business stabilization
- Smaller scale compared to Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron
- Continued dependence on Kioxia joint venture for manufacturing
