## Overview

SanDisk Corporation was founded on June 1, 1988, as SunDisk by Eli Harari, Sanjay Mehrotra, and Jack Yuan. The name "SunDisk" was a portmanteau of co-founder Sanjay's name and "disk." The company changed its name to SanDisk on November 8, 1995, to avoid confusion with Sun Microsystems and went public the same day under the ticker symbol SNDK. SanDisk developed early flash storage technologies including the first flash-based solid-state drive in 1991. The company was acquired by Western Digital in May 2016 for $16 billion, and its flash storage business was spun off again as an independent public company under the Sandisk name in February 2025. Eli Harari served as CEO until 2011, when Sanjay Mehrotra took over until the acquisition. Mehrotra later became CEO of Micron Technology in 2017.

## Detailed findings

### Founding story and founders

SanDisk (originally SunDisk) was founded on June 1, 1988, by three co-founders:

**Eli Harari (Eliyahou Harari):** Born 1945 in Tel Aviv, Israel, to Polish Jewish parents who immigrated to Mandatory Palestine in 1933. He earned his Bachelor's Degree at Manchester University in England and his Master's degree in Physics at Princeton University, studying semiconductors. As an engineering intern at Hughes Aircraft, he played a major role in developing the world's first EEPROM. His first job after graduation was at Intel. He later worked at startups including Synertek, Wafer Scale Integration, and Sun Disk. Harari developed the Floating Gate EEPROM which proved the practicality, reliability, and endurance of semiconductor-based data storage. He holds more than 180 patents. Harari served as SanDisk CEO until 2011 and received the National Medal of Technology and Innovation from President Barack Obama in 2014.

**Sanjay Mehrotra:** Born June 27, 1958, in Kanpur, Uttar Pradesh, India. The youngest of four siblings, his father was a liaison officer in the cotton industry. The family moved to New Delhi when Mehrotra was 10 years old. He transferred from BITS Pilani to the University of California, Berkeley at age 18, earning bachelor's and master's degrees in Electrical Engineering and Computer Science. He later graduated from Stanford University Graduate School of Business Executive Education Program in 2009. Mehrotra holds more than 70 patents and has more than 35 years of experience in the non-volatile semiconductor memory industry. At SanDisk, he progressed through engineering management roles (director of memory design, vice president of product development, senior vice president of engineering, executive vice president and COO) before becoming president and CEO in 2011. After the Western Digital acquisition, Mehrotra became CEO of Micron Technology in May 2017. In 2022, he was elected to the National Academy of Engineering for contributions to nonvolatile memory design.

**Jack Yuan:** The third co-founder of SanDisk. Limited biographical information is available in public sources. Yuan co-founded SunDisk with Harari and Mehrotra in 1988 but maintained a lower public profile compared to his co-founders.

### Key milestones and corporate evolution

**1988:** Founded as SunDisk Corporation on June 1 by Eli Harari, Sanjay Mehrotra, and Jack Yuan.

**1991:** Produced the first flash-based solid-state drive (SSD) in a 2.5-inch hard disk drive form factor for IBM with a 20 MB capacity priced at approximately $1,000.

**1992:** Introduced FlashDisk, a series of PCMCIA/PC Card form factor memory cards that, unlike competing products, did not require a battery to store contents. Production was discontinued in 2002, with the highest capacity model reaching 8 GB.

**1994:** Introduced CompactFlash, one of the first successful flash memory card formats, widely adopted in digital cameras.

**August 1999:** Partnered with Panasonic (Matsushita) and Toshiba to introduce the Secure Digital (SD) card format as a successor to MultiMediaCard. The SD Association was formed in January 2000 to manage the specification.

**November 8, 1995:** Changed name from SunDisk to SanDisk to avoid confusion with Sun Microsystems. Went public via initial public offering the same day, selling 16 million shares at $10.00 per share on Nasdaq under ticker SNDK.

**May 10, 2000:** Announced joint venture with Toshiba Corporation to form a new semiconductor company to produce advanced flash memory, primarily for digital cameras. This Flash Forward venture, with manufacturing facilities in Yokkaichi, Japan, became essential for competing in capital-intensive flash manufacturing.

**2004:** Introduced microSD (originally T-Flash/TransFlash) at CeBIT; adopted as official SD Association format in 2005.

**2005:** Entered the digital audio player market with the SanDisk Sansa e100 series. By 2006, became the second-largest maker of digital audio players in the United States behind Apple.

**October 2005:** Acquired Matrix Semiconductor.

**July 2006:** Acquired M-Systems for approximately $1.5 billion.

**May 2011:** Acquired Pliant Technology (SSD manufacturer) for $327 million.

**2011:** Eli Harari retired as CEO. Sanjay Mehrotra became president and CEO.

**February 2012:** Acquired FlashSoft.

**June 2012:** Acquired Schooner Information Technology (flash-optimized database software).

**July 2013:** Acquired SMART Storage Systems (enterprise SSDs) for $307 million.

**June 2014:** Acquired Fusion-io (enterprise flash for data centers) for $1.1 billion.

**October 21, 2015:** Western Digital announced intent to acquire SanDisk for $19 billion.

**May 12, 2016:** Western Digital completed acquisition of SanDisk at a final valuation of $16 billion after Chinese company Unisplendour pulled out of an agreement to acquire a 15% stake in WD. SanDisk became a subsidiary of Western Digital.

**October 2023:** Western Digital announced intent to spin off its flash storage businesses as a new public company under the SanDisk name.

**February 24, 2025:** Spin-off completed. Sandisk Corporation relisted on Nasdaq under ticker symbol SNDK. All WD-branded flash storage products moved under the SanDisk brand.

**Source:** Wikipedia - SanDisk; Wikipedia - Flash memory

### Leadership transitions

**Eli Harari (1988-2011):** Served as CEO from founding through retirement in 2011. Under his leadership, SanDisk pioneered flash storage technologies, established the Toshiba joint venture, developed key memory card formats, and went public in 1995. Harari's technical background in EEPROM and flash memory provided the company's foundational innovation.

**Sanjay Mehrotra (2011-2016):** Served as president and CEO from 2011 until Western Digital's acquisition in 2016. Mehrotra had been with the company since its founding, progressing through engineering and management roles. His tenure saw major enterprise-focused acquisitions (Pliant, Fusion-io) to expand SanDisk's data center presence. After the acquisition, Mehrotra became CEO of Micron Technology in 2017.

**Post-acquisition leadership (2016-2025):** As a Western Digital subsidiary, SanDisk was integrated into WD's flash storage business. WD continued using the SanDisk brand for consumer flash products.

**David Goeckeler (2025-present):** Following the February 2025 spin-off, Goeckeler serves as CEO of the independent Sandisk Corporation.

### Brand evolution and logo history

**Name evolution:**
- **SunDisk Corporation (1988-1995):** Original founding name, a portmanteau of co-founder Sanjay's name and "disk"
- **SanDisk Corporation (1995-2016):** Changed November 8, 1995 to avoid confusion with Sun Microsystems
- **SanDisk LLC (2016-2024):** Following Western Digital acquisition
- **Sandisk Corporation (2025-present):** Following spin-off from Western Digital, now stylized with camelCase capitalization

**Logo evolution:**
- **First logo (1995-2007):** SanDisk wordmark
- **Second logo (2007-2024):** Updated SanDisk wordmark with different styling
- **Current branding (2025-present):** Sandisk stylization with capital D in camelCase

**Stock market presence:**
- **First tenure (1995-2016):** Nasdaq under ticker SNDK
- **Gap (2016-2025):** Not publicly traded as Western Digital subsidiary
- **Second tenure (2025-present):** Nasdaq relisting under ticker SNDK following spin-off
