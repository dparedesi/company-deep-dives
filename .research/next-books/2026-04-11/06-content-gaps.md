# Content gap analysis: where deep-dive books are missing

Research date: 2026-04-11
Excluded: Palantir (001), Rocket Lab (002), SanDisk (003)

---

## Methodology

**Primary research:** Searched Amazon Books for each candidate company using the URL pattern `amazon.com/s?k=<COMPANY>+company+book&i=stripbooks`. Recorded all results, noting titles, authors, ratings, review counts, publication dates, and pricing. Classified each book as: (a) substantive analytical deep-dive, (b) technical/platform guide, (c) opportunistic self-published (Kindle Unlimited, low review count), or (d) unrelated (books about a different topic sharing the search term).

**Supplementary research:** News coverage analysis from seven outlets (Reuters, TechCrunch, CNBC, Bloomberg, The Verge, Google News, Yahoo Finance) to assess current reader demand. Cross-referenced with existing research files in this directory (02-news-media.md and others from background agents).

**Total Amazon searches conducted:** 17+ across two sessions.

**Key finding:** The "company deep-dive book" category is almost entirely empty. Most existing business books are either founder autobiographies, hagiographic treatments, methodology texts, or low-quality opportunistic KU publications. The gap for analytical, investor-focused deep dives that combine business model analysis with competitive positioning is enormous across virtually every major tech/defense/industrial company.

Anduril is reportedly preparing for an IPO, which drives reader interest. The company is winning major DoD contracts (Counter-UAS, Lattice platform, autonomous underwater vehicles).

**Evidence:**

- The one existing book has 1 review total
- Defense tech is the hottest government contracting sector in 2026
- Anduril's Lattice platform is being deployed at US military bases and border installations
- Palmer Luckey is a household name from the Oculus VR origin story

**Reader profile:** Defense tech enthusiasts, Palantir book readers looking for the next story, investors tracking the defense modernization theme, military/defense professionals.

**Reader profile:** Semiconductor investors, geopolitical analysts, technology strategists, anyone who wants to understand why Taiwan matters to the global economy, CHIPS Act policy followers.

**Suggested angle:** "The company the world cannot afford to lose" -- Morris Chang's story, the geopolitics of semiconductor manufacturing, why every AI company and every military depends on one island, and whether the US can actually build a domestic alternative.

### 9. ARM Holdings (ARM)

The company sits at the intersection of the AI chip boom and the geopolitical semiconductor competition.

**Evidence:**
- Amazon: "The Everything Blueprint" exists (4.4 stars, 76 reviews) but is architecture-focused, not business-focused
- No comprehensive company deep-dive
- ARM designs are in 99% of smartphones
- 2023 IPO with subsequent dramatic stock appreciation
- Expanding from mobile into AI, automotive, and server chips

**Reader profile:** Semiconductor investors, mobile industry analysts, ARM licensees who want to understand the company behind the architecture, anyone tracking the AI chip supply chain.

**Suggested angle:** "The invisible monopoly" -- how a British company designs the chips in your phone, your car, and soon your AI, why ARM's licensing model is one of the most profitable in tech, and whether the SoftBank era will unlock ARM's potential or saddle it with debt.

**Suggested angle:** Only pursue if clearly differentiated: the geopolitical dimensions of EUV, the China export controls, and whether ASML's monopoly is sustainable.
